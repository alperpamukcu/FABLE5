## 1. The five answers

**A drink is worth what the DRINK is worth.** `DrinkPricing` prices a page by the rung it opens
on crossed with how hard it is to make, and nothing multiplies it by the bar's standing any
more. A Vodka & Soda is a three-dollar drink at five stars exactly as it is at none. The way to
earn more is to serve something better — which is the loop the brief asks for, and it also fixes
the thing the book was lying about: the page used to print the sheet while the drinker paid
three times it.

**A night is planned, not rolled.** `DayPlan` decides a night's whole composition up front — how
many covers, how they split across the rungs the bar has opened, how many are easy, ordinary or
hard — and then shuffles only the ORDER. Two nights at the same standing take within a dollar or
two of each other and still play differently. That is *"bir düzen içerisinde rastgele"*, and it
is the direct answer to *"her şeyi rastgele yapıp oyuncuya kötü şans sonucu kötü bir oynanış
sunmak istemiyorum"*: luck decides who walks in when, never what the night is worth.

**The first week is written.** `FirstWeek` authors seven nights as a lesson — one new thing a
night — because a new bar owns six pages and arithmetic on six pages is six drinks in a bag.

**Climbing pays twice.** The rung you just reached is both the dearest pages you own AND the ones
most of the room asks for (45% of covers), so buying up moves the whole night's takings rather
than adding one expensive drink to a night of cheap ones. And the hard column grows with the
house — nothing hard is asked for at zero stars, three covers in ten at five — so the skill the
player built has somewhere to go.

**Nothing costs four hundred dollars.** *"bir kokteyl 400 dolar olmamalı"*: the bands were re-cut
at ×1.5 a rung instead of ×2, the dearest real ticket is about $145, and
`DrinkPricing.CeilingPerDrink` (399) is the backstop at the till for anything nobody has written
yet.

## 3. The first week

Nine covers on the first night, twelve on the seventh, and each night adds exactly one thing.
The sheet total rises every night — a week where night two takes less than night one reads as
going backwards, and a test holds it (`DayPlanTests.TheWrittenWeek_PaysMoreEveryNight`).

The opening night takes **$63** at competent play and **$90** for a player who already knows the
bench, against the brief's *"günlük max kazanç ~$80 / ortalama $60"*. It closes **$40 up** after
rent and stock, which buys the cheapest fitting on the shop's first rung that same night.

**And the worst opening night is survivable.** A bar opens with **$50** (it was $20). Measured: a
first night on which nobody is served at all costs $25–29 — the $14 rent plus a walk-out fee for each
of the five or six drinkers who gave up — and at $20 that put a brand-new bar in debt before it had
poured a drink. At $50 it still closes with $21–25 (`DayPlanTests` holds it).

### The room, night by night

A bar that buys its own way: the rung's better bottles, every page its gate opens, the bottles
those pages need, then the room — the most comfort per dollar while the room is under the
standing plus half a star, the night's one fitting, then the rest cheapest first — keeping
tomorrow's rent less what tonight cleared. Each night is priced from the pages and bottles it
owns and files the lower of the climb, the menu's ceiling and the room (a clean counter).

**The climb is the projection's assumption, not a result:** the service side of every night is the
standing plus 0.135 (`StarsOn`'s slope). A competent night's drinks are worth about 4.0 stars
of service, so in play the drinks bind before the room does; what these tables answer is when the
MONEY buys the house for a bar that climbs at the projection's pace.

## 4. What the shop costs, against what the bar earns

Totalled from `fixtures.json`, `glassware.json`, the locked pages and the stools, each priced at
its own rung the way the shop quotes it (`StarEconomy.PriceAt`):

| rung | things for sale | that rung costs | running |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 0★ | 41 | $1,545 | $1,545 |
| 1★ | 33 | $6,780 | $8,325 |
| 2★ | 34 | $16,434 | $24,759 |
| 3★ | 40 | $31,976 | $56,735 |
| 4★ | 19 | $29,440 | $86,175 |
| 5★ | 1 | $450 | $86,625 |

**Furnishing the ROOM** — the best fitting in each of the 22 slots, as each rung opens it — is
$17,296 of that, and the ground floor's share is **$537**. That is the number that matters for
*"oyuncu oyundan bezdirilmesin"*: a competent player has $422 in the till by the end of the written week
and can have most of the ground floor standing by day ten. Comfort is not the bottleneck it
looks like — those rung-0 fittings are worth 4.55 comfort stars between them — so what actually
gates the climb is SERVICE, which is the player's own hands. That is the right gate.

The shop is bigger than the run. A competent player banks about $33,000 by day 42 against
$86,625 of things to buy, so the till never hugs zero *and* never runs out of decisions —
the two halves of *"Oyuncunun parası hep 0a yakın olmamalı"*.

## 5. Three things worth a decision

**The tip is the whole of the skill reward, and it is large.** At competent play tips are 80% of
the bill; at sharp play they equal it, because the high-roller crowd a good night draws adds a
quarter on top of a full-ceiling tip. That is `ServiceJudge.TipCeiling = 1.15`, set on
2026-09-04 on your own instruction (*"bahşişi arttıracak"*) and measured against the sim then. It
is left alone here — but it means **service, not price, is the bar's main earner**, and it is
worth knowing that deliberately rather than by accident. Cutting it to, say, 0.5 would roughly
halve every figure in §3 and make the star bands the earner instead.

**No page below three stars is rated HARD.** The hard column is 0% of covers at 0★, 1★ and 2★ —
not because `DayPlan` refuses to ask, but because `RecipeDifficulty` rates none of the 25 pages
at ranks 1–14 as hard. The mix table has a hard share from one star up and nothing to put in it.
That is a CONTENT gap, not a code one: a two-star page with three pours and a method on it would
fill it. Until then the reward for hard work only begins at three stars.

**The 1★ rung is thin.** Four pages, against ten on the ground floor and eleven at two stars. A
bar's first climb opens the smallest shelf in the book, so `DayPlan`'s page cap spills most of
that rung's share back down to the opening menu. Two or three more rank 9–11 pages would make the
first star feel like the others.

Three things this is built to be, and is:

- **Night one is untouched.** The room the bar opens with carries every kind at zero, so the
  written first week and every figure in §3 are the same with or without the buffs.
- **The room grows into a real share of the night.** About an eighth of the net at one star, a
  third at two, and over two fifths from three stars up. That is the "somewhat dependent" the brief
  asks for: a bar that stops buying fittings keeps working, but falls a long way behind one that
  furnishes as it climbs.
- **It has a ceiling.** Each kind is capped (PRICE 12%, TIP 15%, CROWD 10% …) and the fully
  furnished room lands exactly on every cap, so nothing a player buys later can run the economy away.

A last note on the crowd, because the first reading of this projection got it wrong and it is
worth not getting wrong twice: the broke crowd reads a night's SATISFACTION of 0.125, not the
bar's standing. A brand-new bar stands at zero stars and is **not** broke — a bar that serves a
dreadful night is. Asking the crowd about the standing made the opening week look like a trap it
is not.
